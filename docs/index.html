Weekly / positional options

How an options trade is chosen (a rules screen, not advice; the decision is yours). Every liquid F&O stock with a swing plan is screened on its own. Direction: target first in 33%+ of 10,000 replays and an expectancy of +0.15R+; the same rule not losing money on this stock; the market agrees (a call in a falling Nifty needs a stock stronger than 70% of others, a put a Nifty not rising and a weaker stock); fundamentals not against it; and no results due (implied volatility usually collapses right after results). Expiry: the nearest monthly one 14+ days away that leaves 7 days after the plan's exit. Structure, within your ₹30,000 a trade: a debit spread by default (buy near the money, sell at or before the target; pay at most half the width; reward:risk 1.5+; break-even inside the target; both legs liquid; at most 25% lost to time decay by the estimated day; no OI wall before the sold strike). A single call or put only when it fits the budget and its IV is no higher than the stock's realised volatility. Entry: only after the stock trades through the plan's entry trigger within 3 sessions, and only if the trade still clears every check then. Exit, whichever comes first: +60% of the cost, -10% of it, the stock's target or stop, or 7 days before expiry. At most 3 trades open at once.
The values at the target and stop are Black-Scholes at each strike's own IV on the estimated day; real prices will differ. A spread caps the profit; a bought option can lose its whole cost. Updated 2026-10-30 15:06 IST.
